The region covered by the XAF model comprises the entire African continent. The dominant plate framework is the internal fragmentation of the African plate into two subplates, Nubia to the west and Somalia to the east, separated by the East African Rift System (EARS), an intracontinental divergent boundary about 5,000 km long that traverses the continent from the Afar Depression (Ethiopia) to Mozambique and connects to the south with the Southwest Indian Ridge [@Saria2014EARkinematics; @Poggi2017EAR]. At Afar the EARS forms a triple junction with the Red Sea rift and the Gulf of Aden, which separate Nubia and Somalia from the Arabian plate; Nubia–Somalia separation reaches about 5 mm/yr along the Main Ethiopian Rift, and the drift of Arabia relative to Nubia and Somalia reaches about 20 mm/yr [@Saria2014EARkinematics; @Maestrelli2022]^[Slip and convergence rates are given in mm/yr (millimetres per year); the equivalence in geological units is 1 mm/yr = 1 m/ka, i.e. 1 km/Ma (kilometres per million years).]. The kinematic model of Saria et al., built from GPS and seismic slip vectors, predicts opening rates of 1–4 mm/yr on the western and eastern branches (increasing southward on the former and northward on the latter), 3–6 mm/yr on the central EARS, and about 4–6 mm/yr on the Main Ethiopian Rift, with an extension direction close to N100°E; the same model requires three internal microplates, Victoria, Rovuma and Lwandle, between Nubia and Somalia [@Saria2014EARkinematics]. Ocean-spreading reconstructions between 3.2 and 20 Ma yield a total Nubia–Somalia divergence of 4.2–5.0 mm/yr, stationary since at least 5.2 Ma [@DeMetsMerkouriev2016]. Cenozoic rifting began in the Paleogene, and both branches became active nearly simultaneously around 25 Ma [@Roberts2012EAR; @Poggi2016SSAHARA].

The north of the continent contains a seismic belt more than 5,000 km long, from Morocco to Egypt, controlled by the Nubia–Eurasia interaction, which varies from transpression in the Atlas to transtension toward the east; Africa–Eurasia convergence across the Gibraltar–Sicily sector is oblique, at 3 to 6 mm/yr with an eastward increase [@PoggiEtAl2020; @GEMHazard2023NAFdoc; @MeghraouiPondrelli2012Atlas]. Meghraoui and Pondrelli interpret the belt as a transpressive system with clockwise block rotation (2.24–3.9°/Ma) and a compression-to-strike-slip ratio that grows from 33 % near Gibraltar to 50 % in the Tunisian Atlas, with the Algerian Tell Atlas acting as a restraining bend^[Restraining bend: segment of a strike-slip fault where the curvature of the trace produces localized compression transverse to the fault. [-@MeghraouiPondrelli2012Atlas]] [-@MeghraouiPondrelli2012Atlas]. The Atlas chain extends about 2,000 km as an intracontinental belt formed by inversion of Mesozoic rifts; the High Atlas, Middle Atlas, Anti-Atlas and Saharan Atlas are distinguished, with the Rif as the orogenic system that continues into the Betics and frames the extensional Alboran Sea [@PoggiEtAl2020]. The Algerian Tell Atlas concentrates most of the seismicity of the Maghreb; the Saharan Atlas is less active, and both chains are separated by the relatively aseismic High Plateaux [@PoggiEtAl2020]. In the northeast, Egyptian geodynamics is controlled by Red Sea opening under a transtensive regime: the predominant mechanisms are normal faulting with a strike-slip component that increases toward the Sinai sub-block [@PoggiEtAl2020].

Sub-Saharan Africa is, with the sole exceptions of the EARS and the Cameroon Volcanic Line, a stable intraplate region of low and diffuse seismicity [@Poggi2016SSAHARA]. The eastern and western branches of the rift flank the Tanzania craton, a 2.5–3 Ga nucleus with cold, thick lithosphere of 150–200 km, interpreted as the Victoria microplate; the Kalahari, Congo and West African cratons complete a mosaic of Precambrian nuclei separated by reactivated Pan-African mobile belts that concentrate the intraplate seismicity, with reactivation of pre-existing zones of weakness under ~NW–SE compression documented in West Africa [@Poggi2016SSAHARA; @Dorbath1984Guinea; @Ouattara2019]. The continental seismicity pattern is controlled by the lithospheric and rheological structure, and the elevated region of eastern and southern Africa (~1000 m) constitutes the African Superswell, dynamically supported by a low-velocity mantle anomaly [@Craig2011Africa; @NybladeRobinson1994].

In the face of this diversity —at least five distinguishable seismogenic behaviors: extensional continental rifting with an anomalously large seismogenic thickness in the southern EARS (35–40 km), transpression with thrust and strike-slip faulting in the Atlas, transtension in the northeast, stable cratonic continental crust with spaced intraplate events, and volcano-tectonic and reservoir-induced seismicity— the XAF implementation collapses the continent into only two tectonic region types^[Tectonic region type (TRT): tectonic class to which its own set of ground-motion attenuation relationships is assigned. [@Poggi2016SSAHARA; @Chen2018Regionalization]]: ASC (Active Shallow Crust) and SCC (Stable Continental Crust) [@Biggs2010Karonga; @MeghraouiPondrelli2012Atlas; @Craig2011Africa]. The assignment relies on the global fuzzy-logic tectonic regionalization of Chen et al., with transition zones in NAF and SSA [@Chen2018Regionalization; @PoggiEtAl2020; @GEMHazard2023SSAdoc]. The ground-motion logic tree uses two GMPEs calibrated on Mediterranean active crust and NGA-West2 for ASC and two calibrated on eastern North America for SCC, with zone weights fixed by expert judgment [@Poggi2016SSAHARA; @PoggiEtAl2020; @ChiouYoungs2014; @Akkar2014EMME; @AtkinsonBoore2006; @Pezeshk2011ENA]. The documented simplifications are four: the EARS is modeled with generic ASC GMPEs not specific to rifts, after additional categories were discarded for lack of calibration data; the transpressive Atlas and the transtensive northeast share the same ASC TRT without distinction of faulting style in the attenuation; there is no subduction TRT, since the Hellenic and Calabrian arcs lie outside the region; and the ASC/SCC boundary is fuzzy by design, which smooths without resolving the binary character of the classification [@Poggi2016SSAHARA; @PoggiEtAl2020]. In addition, the seismogenic thickness of the southern rift (35–40 km) contrasts with the shallow depths (5–15 km) of the eastern branch [@Biggs2010Karonga; @Poggi2016SSAHARA].

The adopted source model is the XAF of the GEM global mosaic (snapshot `legacy/v2018.0.5`), which combines the NAF (North Africa, v2017.1.1), SSA (sub-Saharan Africa, v2018.0.1, extended in 2018 with smoothed seismicity and Madagascar) and WAF (six multipoint sources of West Africa with no published documentation located) components [@Pagani2020GEM; @PoggiEtAl2020; @Poggi2017EAR; @Poggi2016SSAHARA; @GEMHazard2023SSAdoc]. The references for each component are Poggi et al. and the NAAFD fault database for NAF, the SSAHARA technical report and its journal version for SSA, and the GEM record for the 2018 update [-@PoggiEtAl2020; @StyronPoggi2018NAAFD; -@Poggi2016SSAHARA; @GEMHazard2023SSAdoc].
