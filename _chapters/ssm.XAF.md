
The seismic source model (SSM) defines the spatial distribution, geometry, and earthquake occurrence rates of all seismogenic sources considered capable of contributing to ground-motion hazard at a site. In the hazard integral ([-@eq-hazard-integral]), the SSM provides the magnitude-frequency distributions $f_{M,s}(m)$, the spatial distributions $f_{\mathbf{R}|M,s}(\mathbf{r}|m)$, the occurrence rates $\nu_0^{(s)}$, and the maximum magnitude $M_{\max}^{(s)}$ for each source $s$ [@Cornell1968; @SSHAC1997]. Epistemic uncertainty in these parameters is captured through the source-model logic tree described in [@sec-ssm].

The XAF model corresponds to the OpenQuake implementation of the probabilistic seismic hazard model for North Africa developed by Poggi et al. (2020) [-@PoggiEtAl2020], assembled under the GEM Global Seismic Hazard Map programme of the Global Earthquake Model Foundation (GEM) [@Poggi2020]. The model domain extends from approximately $19.5^\circ$W to $52.9^\circ$E in longitude and from $28.7^\circ$S to $38.5^\circ$N in latitude, covering North Africa, West Africa, and parts of sub-Saharan Africa; the GEM documentation describes the regional versions of the Northern Africa (NAF) and Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) models incorporated in the implementation [@GEMHazard2023NAFdoc; @GEMHazard2023SSAdoc].

The inventory comprises 255 sources of two types: 115 simple fault sources (`simpleFaultSource`) and 140 multi-point sources (`multiPointSource`).^[A multi-point source gathers point sources on a mesh and retains one magnitude-frequency distribution per node [@OpenQuakeHazardlib326].] Sources are classified under two tectonic region types (TRTs)^[In OpenQuake, the TRT associates each source with the set of ground-motion models (GMMs) defined for that setting in the ground-motion logic tree [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326; @OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].]: Active Shallow Crust (ASC, 150 sources) and Stable Continental Crust (SCC, 105 sources). Within the fault group, 69 sources are ASC and 46 are SCC; within the multi-point group, 81 are ASC and 59 are SCC. The minimum magnitude adopted for hazard calculations is $M_w$ 4.0 for multi-point sources — ranging from 4.0 to 4.5 depending on the source group — and $M_w$ 6.05 for simple fault sources. Fault source geometries and kinematics derive from the GEM North Africa Active Fault Database (NAAFD) compiled by Styron and Poggi (2018) [-@Styron2018].

The source-model logic tree encodes epistemic uncertainty through five branch sets that produce $2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 162$ end-branches.^[Each branch carries an alternative and a relative weight within its set. The weight of each path is the product of the weights of its branches and determines its contribution to the statistical results of the calculation [@OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].] The first branch set (`sourceModel`) selects the seismicity representation for all sources: the `naf_faults` branch (weight 0.50) combines the explicit fault sources with the collapsed multi-point grids, whereas the `naf_smooth` branch (weight 0.50) replaces the explicit faults with a purely smoothed-seismicity representation. The second and fifth branch sets apply relative $M_{\max}$ perturbations (type `maxMagGRRelative`) of $+0.2$, $0.0$, and $-0.2$, with weights 0.25, 0.50, and 0.25, to the West African multi-point sources `MPS-1` through MPS-6 (set `mmax_waf`) and to the North African smoothed-seismicity sources SC_1 through SC_54 (set `naf_m`), respectively. The third and fourth branch sets apply relative Gutenberg–Richter $b$-value perturbations (type `bGRRelative`) of $+0.05$, $0.0$, and $-0.05$, with the same weight distribution, to the WAF group (set `bval_waf`) and the NAF group (set `naf_b`), respectively; the perturbation structure of the NAF group is identical to that of the WAF group, and the weights within each set sum to unity [@PoggiEtAl2020; @GEMHazard2023NAFdoc].

Three magnitude-frequency distribution (MFD) types^[An MFD assigns annual occurrence rates to magnitude intervals. In a truncated Gutenberg–Richter distribution, parameter $a$ controls the cumulative activity level and $b$ the relative decrease in frequency with increasing magnitude, within the $M_{\min}$ and $M_{\max}$ bounds [@Weichert1980; @OpenQuakeHazardlib326].] characterise recurrence, each associated with a distinct source group. The 115 simple fault sources employ the incremental MFD (`incrementalMFD`), which specifies occurrence rates as discrete values per 0.1-magnitude bin starting at $M_{\min} = 6.05$, with no parametric $a$- or $b$-values; this formulation directly encodes the expected annual rate at each magnitude level and admits non-parametric shapes consistent with activity rates estimated from fault geometry and slip-rate data. The 86 multi-point sources with truncated Gutenberg–Richter MFDs (`multiMFD:truncGutenbergRichterMFD`) — comprising the NAF smoothed-seismicity sources SC_1 through SC_54 and the WAF sources `MPS-1` through MPS-6 — are parameterised by an $a$-value ranging from 3.13 to 5.45 and a $b$-value ranging from 0.93 to 1.16, within explicit $M_{\min}$ and $M_{\max}$ bounds; their epistemic uncertainty in $b$ corresponds to the $\pm 0.05$ perturbations of the third and fourth branch sets described above. The 54 background multi-point sources (BG_1 through BG_54) employ the arbitrary MFD (`multiMFD:arbitraryMFD`), which specifies rates at arbitrary, non-uniformly spaced magnitude points without an underlying parametric model; no $a$- or $b$-values are defined for them either [@PoggiEtAl2020].

Maximum magnitude ($M_{\max}$) defines the upper bound of each source's MFD and controls the tail of the hazard curve. For simple fault sources, base $M_{\max}$ values range from 6.05 to 7.75; because the incremental MFD directly encodes rates per magnitude bin, $M_{\max}$ is implicit as the highest-magnitude bin of each source, and its low and high epistemic bounds equal the base value, so no additional $M_{\max}$ uncertainty exists beyond the source-model selection of the first branch set. The Arzew Fault (FL_119, ASC) carries the highest value in the group, $M_{\max}$ 7.75. For multi-point sources, base values range from 5.25 to 9.00, and epistemic uncertainty corresponds to the $\pm 0.2$ perturbations of the `mmax_waf` and `naf_m` branch sets described above. The collapsed SSA sub-group sources carry identical base, low, and high values, indicating that their epistemic $M_{\max}$ uncertainty was pre-collapsed into the source parameters before model compilation [@PoggiEtAl2020].

Two magnitude-area scaling relationships convert rupture area to moment magnitude, assigned by source type. The 115 simple fault sources use the `Leonard2014_Interplate` relationship of Leonard (2014) [-@Leonard2014], and the 140 multi-point sources use the `WC1994` relationship of Wells and Coppersmith (1994) [-@WellsCoppersmith1994]; both groups adopt a rupture aspect ratio of 2.0. OpenQuake uses these relationships to determine rupture dimensions during hazard calculation.

Seismogenic depth parameters define the vertical extent within which ruptures are generated. All sources share an upper seismogenic depth of 0.0 km. For simple fault sources, the lower seismogenic depth ranges from 1.7 km for very shallow structures to 25.0 km for crustal-scale faults, and rupture geometry derives from the fault model without an explicit hypocentral distribution. Multi-point sources are assigned a deeper seismogenic base: collapsed WAF sources extend to 45.0 km and collapsed SSA sources to 40.0 km, reflecting the broader depth distribution of the smoothed background seismicity across the domain. For the WAF multi-point sources (`MPS-1` through MPS-6), the hypocentral depth distribution is explicitly defined with four discrete levels — 5 km (weight 0.267), 15 km (weight 0.267), 25 km (weight 0.333), and 35 km (weight 0.133) — yielding a mean hypocentral depth of 18.3 km; collapsed SSA sources carry comparable mean hypocentral depths of approximately 18.0 to 19.5 km depending on the sub-group [@PoggiEtAl2020].

Activity rates for fault sources with available slip-rate data may be derived from the seismic-moment balance $\dot{M}_0 = \mu\, A\, S$,^[The seismic moment rate $\dot{M}_0$ is related to the shear modulus $\mu$, the fault area $A$, and the long-term slip rate $S$; this relationship connects geodetic or geologic slip-rate estimates to the moment release rate used in MFD construction.] which connects geodetic or geologic slip-rate estimates to seismic recurrence. The NAAFD documents slip-rate estimates for 15 of the 115 fault sources, encompassing net slip rates, strike-parallel rates, vertical rates, and shortening rates, with magnitudes typically between 0.01 and 2.5 mm/yr^[1 mm/yr = 1 m/ka = 1 km/Ma.] [@Styron2018]. However, these data are not encoded in the OpenQuake source-model XML files: the source-model audit records indicate that no fault source retains slip-rate data in the XML representation [@AuditoriaLocalXAFssm], so the incremental MFD rates of the fault sources were specified independently of the NAAFD slip-rate constraints at the OpenQuake implementation stage [@PoggiEtAl2020].
