The Australian instrumental record covers just over a century [@Leonard2008Catalogo]; pre-instrumental historical seismicity relies on chronicles and macroseismic effects since the 19th century, such as the Tasman Sea sequences [@MichaelLeiba1989Tasmania]. The model's reference catalogue is the NSHA23-Cat (*National Seismic Hazard Assessment Earthquake Hypocentre Catalogue*), which is used to determine the occurrence rates of all area and gridded-seismicity source models of continental Australia [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; @AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Cat]. It is compiled from the Gary Gibson catalogue (GG-Cat, a multi-observatory compilation curated since the 1970s) —the primary source of the NSHA18-Cat— augmented with solutions from Geoscience Australia's National Earthquake Alerts Centre (NEAC) through the end of 2022; this update adds, among others, the 2018 Lake Muir sequence ($M_W$ 5.3 and 5.2), the 2019 offshore Broome earthquake ($M_W$ 6.6), the 2019 offshore Carnarvon earthquake ($M_W$ 5.9), the 2019 Tennant Creek earthquake ($M_W$ 5.0), the 2021 Woods Point earthquake ($M_W$ 5.9) and the 2021 Marble Bar earthquake ($M_W$ 5.3) [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23].

All magnitudes in the NSHA23-Cat are expressed uniformly in $M_W$ using Australia-specific conversion equations updated for the model, applicable to $M_L$, $m_b$ and $M_S$ [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; @AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Cat]. The $M_L$–$M_W$ relation is non-linear and was derived with simulated Wood-Anderson seismograms (gain 2,800): for moderate to large earthquakes (4.0 < $M_W$ < 6.0), $M_W$ is approximately 0.3 units smaller than $M_L$, and for the catalogue as a whole the $M_L$ adjustment and the conversion to $M_W$ reduce by more than 50 % the number of events above magnitude 5.0 [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The original $M_L$ values were additionally recomputed for events since 2010, with a net increase of ~0.1 units from the Wood-Anderson calibration change [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23].

For catalogue declustering, the Leonard (2008) time and distance windows were applied, modified following Burbidge (2012) to account for the prolonged aftershock sequences proposed by Stein and Liu (2009) after large stable-continent earthquakes; the algorithm was applied to the original magnitudes ($M_X$) and left 9,081 independent earthquakes out of a total of 15,373 in the full NSHA23-Cat [@Leonard2008Catalogo; @Burbidge2012NSHM12; -@SteinLiu2009; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The removed dependent events of $M_W$ ≥ 6.0 belong to the late-19th-century Tasman Sea sequence and to the 1988 Tennant Creek sequence; other moderate events belong to the 1941 Simpson Desert sequence [@MichaelLeiba1989Tasmania; @StewartDenham1974Simpson; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The model additionally introduces the truncated declustering window^[The truncated declustering window limits to 13 years the maximum duration during which a later event can be classified as an aftershock and removed from the catalogue; set by expert elicitation (weight 0.48 in favour of truncation, 0.52 against), it reintroduces 171 earthquakes, most of them $M_W$ < 4.0 [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23].]; to retain the hazard from the two large Tennant Creek aftershocks ($M_W$ 6.2 and 6.3), their moment was added to that of the mainshock ($M_W$ 6.6), giving an equivalent magnitude of $M_W$ 6.76 [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23].

For the northern plate margin, version 9.1 of the ISC-GEM global instrumental catalogue is used (complete through December 2018), extended with solutions from the Geoscience Australia catalogue through December 2022, with all magnitudes in $M_W$ [@StorchakEtAl2015ISCGEM; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The completeness magnitude considered for the region of interest is $M_W$ 5.75 from 1975 [@AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data]. The declustering of this catalogue, calibrated for plate margins and not for the stable continent, used the Gardner and Knopoff (1974) algorithm implemented in the GEM HMTK, which removed nearly 50 % of the events [-@GardnerKnopoff1974; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23].

The canonical events of the record cover the regimes present in the model. In unextended cratonic crust, the triple 1988 Tennant Creek sequence stands out ($M_W$ 6.6 mainshock, with aftershocks of $M_W$ 6.2 and 6.3 within 12 hours and three independent surface ruptures), the largest instrumental sequence in Australia, with aftershocks still active decades later ($M_W$ 5.0 in 2019) [@ChoyBowman1990Tennant; @Bowman1992Tennant; @CroneEtAl1992Tennant; @KingEtAl2019Rupturas; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The 1941 Simpson Desert sequence ($M_W$ 5.6, 5.9 and 6.5, with an additional $M_W$ 5.6 event in 1972) is the largest pre-1960 sequence of the cratonic interior, with no known surface rupture [@StewartDenham1974Simpson; @KingEtAl2019Rupturas; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The 1941 Meeberrie earthquake, one of the largest Australian continental earthquakes, was downgraded from a historical value of 7.2 to $M_W$ 6.3 by the catalogue revision, a case that illustrates the effect of magnitude homogenization [@KingEtAl2019Rupturas; @AllenEtAl2018NSHA18Cat]. Petermann Ranges 2016 ($M_W$ 6.0–6.1) produced a ~15–21 km surface rupture in cratonic crust, with documented scarp paleoseismology and geomorphic longevity [@GoldEtAl2019Petermann; @HejraniTkalcic2018Petermann; @KingEtAl2021Petermann].

In the non-cratonic and extended crust of the southwest and southeast, Meckering 1968 ($M_W$ 6.5–6.6) was the first Australian earthquake with documented surface rupture (~37 km, reverse-dextral, up to 2.5 m of displacement) and prompted the first Australian seismic code (AS 2121-1979) [@GordonLewis1980Meckering; @KingEtAl2019Rupturas; @WilsonEtAl2008AS2121]. Newcastle 1989 ($M_W$ 5.4; $M_L$ 5.6) was one of the most significant natural disasters in Australian history, with 13 deaths, more than 160 hospitalized, about 50,000 damaged buildings and a damage bill estimated at more than A$4,000 M according to the Insurance Council of Australia [@GA2019Newcastle; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. The Burakin sequence (WA, 2000–2002) produced a seismic swarm of about 18,000 events with three events of $M_L$ ≥ 5.0, mixed mechanisms (reverse, strike-slip, normal) and depths under 5 km [@Leonard2002Burakin]. Woods Point 2021 ($M_W$ 5.9) is the recent earthquake of the continental southeast with well-recorded aftershocks and the subject of stochastic ground motion simulation [@NinisEtAl2021WoodsPoint; @TangMai2023WoodsPoint]. In the strike-slip regime of the Northwest Shelf, the 2019 offshore Broome earthquake ($M_W$ 6.6), of pure strike-slip mechanism, is the largest recent event and consistent with the margin regime [@WilliamsEtAl2019Broome; @Clark2023FSM23].

Only nine events in the NSHA23-Cat have produced documented scarps [@ClarkEtAl2020LakeMuir; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]; the systematic re-analysis of the primary literature (67 publications) covers ten of the eleven historical earthquakes with known surface rupture in Australia, which occurred between 1968 and 2018 with $M_W$ between 4.7 and 6.6 [@KingEtAl2019Rupturas; @ClarkEtAl2020LakeMuir]. Nine of the fifteen historical earthquakes with documented surface rupture globally in SCR crust occurred in the unextended Precambrian crust of Australia, and all historical Australian ruptures are located in that type of crust [@ClarkEtAl2020LakeMuir]. The large events of Precambrian crust without surface rupture (Simpson Desert 1941, Lake Mackay 1970, Meeberrie 1941) have estimated depths of 7–33 km, versus less than 6 km for the rupturing events, which indicates a hazard source not capturable by active-fault inventories [@KingEtAl2019Rupturas].

The model documentation explicitly declares two complementary catalogue biases: detection incompleteness for small magnitudes, mitigated with multi-corner completeness models by zone [@AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data], and the short observation window relative to seismic cycles of thousands to tens of thousands of years, such that smoothed seismicity assumes a stationarity that the paleoseismic record contradicts [@ClarkMcPhersonVanDissen2012; @ClarkEtAl2014Ernabella; @ClarkEtAl2015Cadell; @AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]. In Kalgoorlie, rates are possibly affected by mining activity and the domain b values may not be appropriate there (Hudyma et al., 2003; Allen et al., 2020b; cited in [-@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23]). The catalogue Record (NSHA23-Cat) appears as *in press* in the references of the model Records, so the count figures and magnitude conversions reported here come from Record 2023/53 and remain pending comparison with the definitive catalogue publication [-@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; -@AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Cat].
