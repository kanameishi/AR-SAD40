The seismic design of tailings storage facilities follows the two-level performance philosophy consolidated by dam-safety practice — an Operating Basis Earthquake (OBE) that the facility must withstand without operational interruption and a Safety Evaluation Earthquake (SEE) under which it must not release tailings — formalized by ICOLD Bulletin 148 for embankment structures [@ICOLD2016; @ANCOLD2012] and expressed by the USACE regulation ER 1110-2-1806 in terms of the OBE and the Maximum Design Earthquake [@USACE1995].

Three international frameworks implement this philosophy for tailings facilities on a consequence basis: the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management [@gistm2020], the Canadian Dam Association guidelines [@cda2021; @CDA2019], and the Australian National Committee on Large Dams [@ancold2019]. The consequence class — from Low to Extreme — reflects the potential severity of a failure in terms of population at risk, anticipated loss of life, and environmental and socio-economic impacts, and governs the stringency of the seismic criteria; the classes and the design-earthquake annual exceedance probabilities (AEPs) are tabulated in Tables [-@tbl-gistm], [-@tbl-cda] and [-@tbl-ancold].

The frameworks differ in structure and in their evolution across the lifecycle. GISTM specifies a single design ground motion for each consequence class and lifecycle stage, with the Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) evaluated in parallel and governing whenever it produces the higher demand [-@gistm2020]; CDA adopts the SEE as the design basis, admits the MCE as a declared alternative to the probabilistic level for high-consequence facilities, and allows risk-based departures for lower classes during transitional closure stages provided the post-closure requirements are ultimately met [-@cda2021]; ANCOLD applies a uniform OBE of 1/500 AEP together with an SEE that ranges from 1/1,000 AEP up to the greater of the MCE and the 1/10,000 AEP event (84th percentile) for Extreme-class facilities [-@ancold2019], in line with mining-sector regulatory guidance that assigns design return periods of 10,000 years or longer to extreme-consequence structures [@UBCThesis]. During construction, partial-height embankments are verified at least at the OBE level, with SEE criteria for high-consequence facilities or high-seismicity regions; during operation — the stage with the largest stored volumes and the highest risk profile — the differentiation by consequence class is greatest, with operational AEPs ranging from 1/200 (GISTM, Low) or 1/100 (CDA) to 1/10,000 for Extreme facilities; and no uniform post-closure criterion exists: GISTM sets a 1/10,000 AEP for all classes during passive-care post-closure [@gistm2020], CDA retains consequence-category-dependent criteria through closure and passive care — passive care is not equivalent to abandonment of the facility, and any risk-based transition must be justified [@cda2021; @CDA2019] — and ANCOLD adopts for post-closure the SEE corresponding to the category and requires it to be reassessed at closure when the hazard, the condition of the dam, or its consequences change [@ancold2019].

Design ground motions for slopes are reported here by operation, closure, and post-closure stage; these labels do not constitute universal normative equivalences with the OBE, the SEE, or the PCE, and the applicable correspondence is interpreted by standard and consequence class.
