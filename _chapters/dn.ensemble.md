The horizontal pseudostatic coefficient is derived in this assessment from the allowable permanent displacement $D_a$ of the potentially sliding mass [@BrayTravasarou2009; @MacedoEtAl2018]. The seismic demand and the fundamental periods established in the preceding chapters allow the $D_n (k_y)$ relations to be computed; their inversion yields $k_{\max}$, and its normalization by the PGA transformed to the $V_{S30}$ of each geometry–material combination yields $k_h$. The result traceably links the deformation objective to the horizontal action employed in the stability verification.

The yield acceleration $a_y$ represents the acceleration level that mobilizes the available resistance along the potential failure mechanism, and its dimensionless form is $k_y=a_y/g$. For each allowable displacement and target exceedance probability, the analysis determines the $k_y$ threshold that satisfies the performance criterion and expresses it as a coefficient for the pseudostatic limit-equilibrium verification.

In Newmark's kinematic idealization, relative motion begins when the driving acceleration exceeds $a_y$ and continues until the relative velocity returns to zero; the permanent displacement corresponds to the sum of those episodes over the duration of the earthquake motion [@Newmark1965]. The adopted empirical relations represent the accumulated displacement statistically through rigid-block and flexible-block idealizations.

The displacement calculation uses the seismic demand and the fundamental periods established in Chapter [-@sec-slope_periods]. For each geometry, material and seismic demand — the probabilistic spectrum of each evaluated return period and the MCE scenario — a joint sample of the intensity measures required by the rigid-block and flexible formulations is constructed. That sample is common to all models, so their differences represent the displacement formulation rather than incompatible seismic demands.

The probabilistic input of each return period is the reference-rock UHS, which for 10,000 years is the MDE, and the MCE input is the rock spectrum of each scenario; both retain the complete distribution of the rock demand. Both are defined for $V_{S30}=V_{\mathrm{ref}}=760$ m/s. For each geometry and material scenario, the spectral ordinates and the PGA are transformed to the corresponding $V_{S30}$ through the `ST20` amplification model [@Stewart2020; @Hashash2020].

The joint sample comprises PGA, $S_a (1.3 T_s)$, and $S_a (1.5 T_s)$ at the site condition of each scenario. The three measures are represented through empirical marginals in $\ln S_a$ and a Gaussian copula with the full inter-period correlation matrix of Baker and Jayaram [-@BakerJayaram2008]. The samples are recentered to preserve the mean of each amplified marginal. In each realization, the three intensities remain paired; each displacement formulation uses the measures entering its regression.

The Arias intensity used by `JB07` and `SR08` is estimated for each realization from the PGA [@Verri2023a]. The evaluation applies this relation deterministically: each PGA realization determines a single value of $I_A$, and the displacement sample propagates the PGA variability.

For each scenario, 30 values of $k_y$ are evaluated, logarithmically distributed between 0.01 and $\max (\overline{\mathrm{PGA}},0.80)$, where $\overline{\mathrm{PGA}}$ is the mean site-condition PGA of the scenario. That interval establishes the computed support of the $D_n (k_y)$ relations.
