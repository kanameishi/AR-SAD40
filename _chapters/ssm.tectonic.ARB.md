The Arabian Peninsula forms a microcontinent that behaves as a plate of its own, wedged between southern Eurasia and Africa [@GEMHazardTeamARB]. The Arabian plate moves northward relative to Eurasia at about 20 mm/yr, a motion that produces continental convergence and reverse faulting in the Zagros and eastern Anatolia fold belts; to the southeast, the boundary continues as subduction of oceanic crust beneath Iran and western Pakistan [@GEMHazardTeamARB; @GEMHazardTeamMIE]^[Slip and convergence rates are given in mm/yr (millimetres per year); the equivalence in geological units is 1 mm/yr = 1 m/ka, i.e. 1 km/Ma (kilometres per million years).]. GNSS measurements show that the plate behaves as essentially rigid, with resolvable internal deformation below ~1.5 mm/yr and the only marginal exception of N–S shortening of up to 1.5 mm/yr in the Palmyride mountains of Syria [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate]. Geodetic Euler vectors quantify the boundaries: Arabia–Nubia (0.37°/Ma), Arabia–Somalia (0.40°/Ma), Arabia–Sinai (0.15°/Ma), and Arabia–Eurasia (0.404°/Ma) [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate]. Global reference models and regional GNSS solutions are consistent with these rates, and the plate kinematic model indicates that the relative Arabia–Nubia, Arabia–Somalia, and Arabia–Eurasia motions have remained unchanged since at least 11, 15, and 24 Ma, respectively [@DeMets2010MORVEL; @Reilinger2006GPS; @Viltres2022ArabianPlate].

To the south and west, Arabia separates from northeast Africa and generates oceanic spreading centers in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, two arms of a ridge–ridge–ridge triple junction whose third arm is the East African Rift [@GEMHazardTeamARB]. Red Sea opening increases southward, from ~7 mm/yr in the north to ~17 mm/yr in the southern Red Sea, whereas in the Gulf of Aden it grows from west to east, from ~15.2 ± 0.2 mm/yr in the Gulf of Tadjoura to ~22.9 ± 0.2 mm/yr near the Aden–Owen–Carlsberg triple junction [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate; @Viltres2022ArabianPlate]. In Afar, total spreading rates are ~18 mm/yr (Nubia–Arabia), ~16 mm/yr (Somalia–Arabia), and ~6 mm/yr (Nubia–Somalia) [@McClusky2010Afar]. From basin widths and the present geodetic rate, the onset of rifting in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden is estimated at 24 ± 2.2 Ma, approximately synchronous along the entire system [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate]. The boundary continues in the Indian Ocean as the Owen fracture zone, with right-lateral strike-slip of 2.5–3.2 ± 0.5 mm/yr, increasing from north to south, and 1–2 mm/yr of extension [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate].

To the north, Arabia–Eurasia convergence is absorbed mostly by the Bitlis–Zagros fold-and-thrust belt [@GEMHazardTeamMIE]. Geodetic convergence rates increase from west to east, from ~16–18 mm/yr at the northern Arabian promontory to ~25–26 mm/yr at the longitude of eastern Iran, and active shortening across the Zagros reaches ~10 mm/yr in the southeast and decreases to 4–7 mm/yr toward the northwest [@Vernant2004IranGPS; @Hessami2006Zagros; @Walpersdorf2006Zagros; @Khorrami2019Iran; @Allen2013Zagros]. Zagros seismicity concentrates between the deformation front and the 1250 m topographic contour, with focal depths generally shallower than 20 km, and the obliquity of convergence is partitioned into reverse displacement on belt-parallel thrusts and right-lateral strike-slip on the Main Recent Fault; a substantial fraction of the shortening is aseismic, absorbed by folding and ductile flow [@Talebian2004Zagros; @Allen2013Zagros; @Nissen2011Zagros]. To the east, the boundary continues as the Makran subduction zone (~1000 km, from the Strait of Hormuz to southern Pakistan), where Arabian oceanic crust subducts beneath Eurasia beneath an accretionary prism ~7–7.5 km thick [@Byrne1992Makran; @GEMHazardTeamMIE]. Estimated convergence rates vary by method: ~18–27 mm/yr from GPS versus ~35–40 mm/yr from plate kinematic models; a coupling model (locking assumed to 30 km, mean dip 11°) yields 20.4 mm/yr on the western segment and 32.6 mm/yr on the eastern one, with partial locking of the interface [@Vernant2004IranGPS; @Masson2005Makran; @Byrne1992Makran; @DeMets2010MORVEL; @Penney2017Makran].

To the northwest, the African (Sinai) and Arabian plates are separated by the Dead Sea Fault or Dead Sea Transform (DST), a left-lateral transform system more than 1000 km long with published rates of 2–8 mm/yr that connects the Red Sea spreading center with the eastern Anatolia collision zone and has accumulated ~105 km of displacement since the middle Miocene [@GEMHazardTeamMIE; @Wechsler2014DSF]. South of the Lebanese restraining bend, the geodetic rate is 4.5–4.7 ± 0.2 mm/yr left-lateral with 0.8–1.1 ± 0.3 mm/yr of extension, and rates decrease from south to north, from ~5 mm/yr to ~2 mm/yr, with a mean locking depth of ~11 km [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate; @LeBeon2008DSF; @Meghraoui2015DSF]. The interior of the peninsula shows no GNSS-resolvable internal deformation and constitutes a stable continental region (SCR)^[Stable continental region: an intraplate domain characterized by very low deformation rates and diffuse seismicity, with no association to active plate boundaries [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate].] [@ArRajehi2010ArabianPlate]. Intraplate basaltic provinces (harrats) have developed over the western Precambrian shield during the last ~30 Ma, associated with Red Sea opening; the 1256 CE Al Madinah eruption indicates that some remain active, and the 2009 Harrat Lunayyir swarm documented a dike intrusion with moderate seismicity and surface deformation [@Pallister2010Lunayyir; @Camp1987Madinah; @Jonsson2012InSAR].

These processes define the seismogenic domains of the region. A domain of active shallow crust groups the DST, the Zagros, the Makran crust, and the internal faults of Iran, with focal depths typically shallower than 20–25 km in the Zagros and magnitudes in the record reaching $M_S$ 7.8 in the large historical DST events and $M_W$ 7.3 in the instrumental record of the Zagros [@Meghraoui2015DSF; @Nissen2019SarpolZahab; @Danciu2017EMMESources]. Makran subduction constitutes the region's only subduction domain, with interface seismicity and seismicity of the subducting Arabian plate; the interface produced the 1945 earthquake ($M_W$ 8.1), the largest instrumental event recorded on it [@Byrne1992Makran; @GEMHazardTeamMIE; @Danciu2017EMMESources]. The peninsular interior constitutes a stable continental region, with diffuse seismicity and moderate magnitudes, whereas the Red Sea and the harrat volcanic zones form an extensional domain associated with rift opening [@GEMHazardTeamARB].
