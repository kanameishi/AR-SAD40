
The factor $F$ of [-@eq-site-af] follows the ergodic site-amplification framework developed for central and eastern North America. Stewart *et al.* [-@Stewart2020] establish the linear response and the reference-condition change; Hashash *et al.* [-@Hashash2020] formulate the intensity-dependent nonlinear modification. The model is expressed with respect to a 3000 m/s hard-rock condition; to transform an input at $V_{\mathrm{ref}}=760\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the calculation applies the exact difference between the composite model evaluated at the target condition and at the reference condition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\ln F}&=f-f_{\mathrm{ref}},\\
f&=\ln F_{760}+\ln F_V+\ln F_{nl}.
\end{aligned}
$${#eq-site-decomp}

$V_{S30}$ is the travel-time-averaged shear-wave velocity in the uppermost 30 m, $T_n$ is the oscillator period, and $pga^*$ is the input PGA. Here $f$ groups the three log-amplification components evaluated at the target condition, for each period and input intensity, and $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the same function evaluated at the reference condition $V_{\mathrm{ref}}$. In each evaluation of the composite model, $F_{\mathrm{comp}}=F_{760}F_VF_{nl}$; the factor applied from 760 m/s is therefore the ratio between $F_{\mathrm{comp}}$ at the target condition and at the reference condition.

The linear term $F_V$ represents the stiffness change with respect to the reference condition and varies with $V_{S30}$ and $T_n$; it equals unity at 760 m/s and decreases gradually toward stiffer conditions. The term $F_{760}$ links the 760 m/s reference to the model's native 3000 m/s hard rock; it depends on the period, generally exceeds unity, and weights reference profiles with impedance contrast and with velocity gradient. The difference in [-@eq-site-decomp] preserves that weighting in both evaluations, including its transition for velocities below 600 m/s.

The term $F_{nl}$ represents the variation of amplification with ground-motion intensity and depends on $V_{S30}$, $T_n$, and the input PGA $pga^*$. The formulation uses a period-dependent reference —760 m/s for $T_n<0.4$ s and 3000 m/s for longer periods— and converts the input PGA at 760 m/s to the hard-rock reference through $pga^*_{3000}=pga^*_{760}/2.275$ [@Stewart2020; @Hashash2020]. At low intensities the response approaches the linear regime; as $pga^*$ increases, the term reproduces the reduction in amplification associated with nonlinear soil response. The balance between $F_V$ and $F_{nl}$ makes it possible to represent amplification at intermediate and long periods and reduction at short periods for softer soils, while the transformation approaches unity when the target condition coincides with the reference.

The dispersion of [-@eq-site-sigma-decomp] accompanies the same evaluation by period, velocity, and intensity. $\sigma_L$ represents the dispersion of the linear term; $\sigma_I$ enters the conversion from the 3000 m/s reference and vanishes when the input is already expressed at 760 m/s; and $\sigma_{NL}=\sigma_{f2}\ln[(pga^*_{3000}+f_3)/f_3]$ represents the dispersion of the nonlinear term. The coefficients and the transitions with $T_n$ and $V_{S30}$ are those tabulated by Stewart *et al.* [-@Stewart2020].

The 760 and 3000 m/s references serve distinct roles in the calculation. The former corresponds to the B/C boundary of the NEHRP classification and is a customary reference condition in United States ground-motion models and design criteria; moreover, much of the recorded data supporting these models concentrates approximately between 200 and 800 m/s. The latter represents the shallow crystalline basement of central and eastern North America used by NGA-East and by regional hard-rock assessments. Stewart *et al.* [-@Stewart2020] establish the conversion between the two references while maintaining compatibility with the empirical data and the site classes. The model is calibrated primarily for $V_{S30}$ between approximately 200 and 1500--2000 m/s and applies gradual transitions toward the limits of its domain.
