

The Stable Continental Crust (SCC) ground-motion logic tree applies to all sources classified as the Stable Continental Crust tectonic region type. The tree comprises six branches with a quasi-uniform weighting scheme: five branches each carry a weight of $0.16667$ and the sixth (`CollapsedNGAEastUSGS`) carries the complementary residual weight of $0.16665$, ensuring the total sums to exactly $1.00000$. The SCC tree is the only one among the four tectonic region types in this assessment that does not achieve exact uniform weighting, owing to the rounding required by the six-branch structure. The SCC tree contains fewer branches than the ASC, SIF, and SIS trees, reflecting the more limited number of GMPEs calibrated directly for stable continental regions.

The six branches are documented below, with their assigned weight and calibration context:

* **`Atkinson2008prime`** (weight: 0.16667). This is OpenQuake's implementation of the Atkinson referenced empirical relation for Eastern North America with the adjustment published by Atkinson and Boore [-@AtkinsonBoore2011]. The model applies to the stable continental regime.

* **`AtkinsonBoore2006Modified2011`** (weight: 0.16667). The Atkinson and Boore (2006) stochastic-based GMPE for Eastern North America [@AtkinsonBoore2006], subsequently modified in 2011 to incorporate magnitude-dependent stress-drop scaling that improved agreement with newer observational data. The model is calibrated for the stable continental ENA regime.

* **`PezeshkEtAl2011`** (weight: 0.16667). A hybrid empirical GMPE for Central and Eastern North America (CENA) by Pezeshk, Zandieh, and Tavakoli (2011), published in the Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America [@Pezeshk2011]. The model combines NGA-West1 model predictions with stochastic ground-motion simulations adjusted to ENA source and attenuation parameters, spanning $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.0 at rupture distances up to approximately 1,000 km, reflecting the long-range attenuation characteristics of stable continental crust.

* **`ESHM20Craton`** (weight: 0.16667). The craton-specific GMPE developed by Weatherill and Cotton (2020) for the 2020 European Seismic Hazard Model (ESHM20), published in the Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering [@WeatherillCotton2020]. The model is constructed through a scaled backbone approach adapting Eastern North American GMPEs to characterize epistemic uncertainty in European craton ground motion, designed for stable cratonic environments such as the East European Craton.

* **`SomervilleEtAl2009YilgarnCraton_SS14`** (weight: 0.16667). A ground-motion model by Somerville et al. (2009) for rock sites in the Australian Yilgarn Craton, derived from stochastic simulations constrained by regional seismological parameters [@Somerville2009]. The model covers source magnitudes in the range $M_w$ 5.0 to 7.5 and captures enhanced long-period ground motion associated with surface waves in this stable cratonic environment. The `SS14` designation indicates that the standard deviation model has been updated using the Seyhan and Stewart (2014) formulation.

* **`CollapsedNGAEastUSGS`** (weight: 0.16665). A custom GSIM registered via the project's `gmm.gmpe` extension module, extending the OpenQuake `NGAEastUSGSGMPE` base class [@Goulet2017]. This implementation collapses the ensemble of NGA-East USGS ground-motion models into a single GSIM by pre-computing model predictions into a runtime HDF5 lookup table (`NGAEastUSGS_collapsed.hdf5`) and inflating the total standard deviation with a stored between-model epistemic term, thereby preserving the spread of the original multi-model suite within a single logic tree branch. The underlying NGA-East USGS models were developed for Central and Eastern North America (CENA) through a SSHAC Level 3 process, covering $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.2 at distances up to approximately 1,500 km. The collapsed implementation requires only $V_{S30}$ and moment magnitude; distance enters the model internally through the precomputed lookup table.
