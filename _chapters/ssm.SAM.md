The adopted seismic source model is a local adaptation of the regional seismic source model for South America (SAM).^[In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis, a source represents the location, geometry, and recurrence of the earthquakes considered; a rupture is a realization characterized by its magnitude, surface, hypocenter, mechanism, and occurrence [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].] The Global Earthquake Model Foundation (GEM) report documents version v2020.1.0, lists 8 June 2023 as the processing date, and describes a continental-scale model developed from the regional earthquake catalog, active-fault compilations, and tectonic and geodetic information [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. The adopted implementation classifies its sources using the tectonic region type (TRT) codes^[In OpenQuake, the TRT associates each source with the set of ground-motion models (GMMs) defined for that setting in the ground-motion logic tree [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326; @OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].] ASC, SCC, SIF, and SIS. The scientific characterization refers to the documented version; the TRT assignments and inventory values correspond to the adopted implementation.

SAM organizes seismic activity into three components: shallow crustal seismicity, subduction-interface seismicity, and intraslab and deep seismicity.^[Interface seismicity occurs along the contact between the downgoing and overriding plates; intraslab seismicity occurs within the downgoing plate.] The intraslab and deep component includes the deep seismicity of the Bucaramanga seismic nest, a spatial concentration of deep earthquakes in Colombia [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. In the crustal component, distributed seismicity is represented by multi-point sources comprising nodes with spatially varying rates, magnitude–frequency distributions, hypocentral depths, and weighted nodal planes defined by strike, dip, and rake [@GEMHazardTeamSAM; @OpenQuakeHazardlib326]. The crustal component also includes simple fault sources. The interface is represented by complex fault sources defined by three-dimensional surfaces [@GEMHazardTeamSAM; @OpenQuakeSourceModels326]. The intraslab and deep component uses nonparametric sources.^[A nonparametric source explicitly specifies each rupture and its occurrence-probability distribution over the investigation time [@GEMHazardTeamSAM; @OpenQuakeHazardlib326].]

Distributed crustal seismicity is characterized within zones having consistent tectonic and kinematic properties and seismicity rates. Each zone adopts a common characterization of tectonics, mechanisms, and recurrence, and its mesh represents the spatial variation in seismicity rate [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. The regional catalog and geologic and tectonic information support the hypocentral-depth distribution, rupture orientation and style of faulting, magnitude–frequency distribution (MFD), maximum magnitude, and seismogenic thickness.^[Seismogenic thickness is the vertical interval of the crust capable of brittle rupture, bounded by the upper and lower seismogenic depths assigned to the source [@GEMHazardTeamSAM; @OpenQuakeSourceModels326].] The MFD assigns annual occurrence rates to magnitude intervals. In a truncated Gutenberg–Richter distribution, parameter $a$ controls the cumulative activity level and $b$ the relative decrease in frequency with increasing magnitude, within the $M_{\min}$ and $M_{\max}$ bounds [@Weichert1980; @OpenQuakeHazardlib326]. Rates are distributed spatially by smoothing the observed seismicity, which converts the spatial distribution of the earthquake catalog into weights on the mesh and allocates each zone's rate among its nodes [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. Where the mesh overlaps fault sources, SAM sets $M_w$ 6.5 as the upper bound of the distributed-seismicity MFD and the lower bound of the fault-source MFD. This rule allocates the magnitude intervals considered at nodes near the surface projection of a fault between the two representations [@GEMHazardTeamSAM].

Crustal faults are represented by simple fault sources. Their geometry comprises the fault trace, dip, and seismogenic-depth limits; rake, together with strike and dip, establishes the orientation and style of faulting of the ruptures [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326]. For most of these sources, the SAM documentation derives activity from slip rates—the relative displacement accumulated per unit time across the fault—and uses a truncated Gutenberg–Richter distribution [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. A subset uses the Youngs–Coppersmith formulation, which combines a Gutenberg–Richter branch for lower-magnitude seismicity with a characteristic component concentrated around the magnitude associated with the principal rupture of the fault [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. Maximum magnitude is calculated from fault dimensions, and the $b$ value is adopted from the crustal zone containing the structure [@GEMHazardTeamSAM].

The subduction interface is represented by complex fault sources with three-dimensional surfaces defined by top, bottom, and, where applicable, intermediate edges; this geometry represents variations in width, strike, and dip along the surface [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326]. The surface is divided into segments defined from the geometry of the slab, observed seismicity, the history of large earthquakes, convergence, and regional kinematics. Each interface rupture is confined within the corresponding segment boundaries, which establish its maximum spatial extent [@GEMHazardTeamSAM]. The MFDs combine statistics from the observed seismicity with a characteristic component that represents the recurrence of an extensive rupture of the segment. SAM derives the magnitude and rate of this component from the interface area, convergence, and seismic coupling coefficient [@GEMHazardTeamSAM].^[The seismic coupling coefficient is the ratio of the seismic moment release rate to the tectonic moment rate associated with relative plate motion [@ScholzCampos2012].] These estimates are combined with the catalog-based recurrence [@GEMHazardTeamSAM].

Within the intraslab and deep component, the geometry and occurrence distribution of each rupture are specified explicitly before the calculation. The divisions used for the interface are extended into the downgoing plate as units of spatial parameterization, and the ruptures represent the observed seismicity within the continuous volume of the slab [@GEMHazardTeamSAM].
