In this section, historical seismicity denotes the pre-instrumental record characterized by macroseismic intensities and documented chronicles, as opposed to instrumental seismicity recorded by seismographic networks; in the region the compiled historical parametric catalogue goes back to 31 BCE and the systematic instrumental period begins around 1900 [@Erdik2012EMME]. The regional reference catalogue is the one compiled by the EMME project (ECME catalogue), a unified historical and instrumental compilation for the Middle East with magnitudes homogenized to $M_W$ through regional conversion relations derived from events with dual estimates, for example $M_W = 0{,}87\,m_b + 0{,}83$ for $3{,}5 \leq m_b \leq 6{,}0$ and $M_W = 0{,}66\,M_S + 2{,}11$ for $2{,}8 \leq M_S \leq 6{,}1$, comparable to the reference relations of Scordilis [@Zare2014ECME; @Erdik2012EMME; @Scordilis2006]. The main international instrumental sources were NEIC, ISC, and the EHB bulletin, and NOAA for the historical part, complemented with local catalogues from the partner countries [@Erdik2012EMME]. The historical catalogue exceeds 2000 records, of which about 25% lack an intensity or magnitude estimate, and the 1900–2010 instrumental catalogue contains about 6100 records, of which 526 have $M_W \geq 6$, 134 $M_W \geq 6{,}5$, and 41 $M_W \geq 7$ [@Erdik2012EMME]. Completeness is regionally variable and has been explicitly verified only for Makran, where $M_c = 5{,}5$ is suggested from 1920 and $M_c = 4{,}5$ from 1963; the catalogue extends to 2006 [@Zare2014ECME]. The ISC-GEM catalogue, which covers 1904–2014 after the extension project, is used as the global backbone homogenized to $M_W$ [@DiGiacomo2018ISCGEM].

The ARB model was built with a composite catalogue owned by the Saudi Geological Survey, whose datasets are not public; the total number of events, the completeness periods by magnitude threshold, the conversions applied, and the counts before and after declustering could not be verified [@GEMHazardTeamARB]. The smoothed-seismicity analysis for Saudi Arabia documents that the choice of declustering technique is the dominant source of epistemic uncertainty in the result, especially for high hazard levels [@Sokolov2017SaudiPSHA]. Other relevant regional compilations are the Arabian plate catalogue of Deif et al. and the unified Iran catalogue of Karimiparidari et al. [@Deif2017ArabianCatalogue; -@Karimiparidari2013Iran].

Following the selection criterion by seismogenic regime, the selected events of the Dead Sea Transform (ASC) include the large historical earthquakes documented in Roman, Byzantine, and Arab chronicles —363, 749, and 1033 CE, the last with $7{,}0 \leq M_S < 7{,}8$ and surface rupture on the Jordan Valley fault— which anchor the long recurrence of the system [@Meghraoui2015DSF; @Ferry2011JordanValley; @Ambraseys1998EMed]. The 20 May 1202 event on the Yammouneh segment ($M_S$ ~7.5–7.6) produced several kilometres of surface rupture and 1.6 m of coseismic displacement measured at Vadum Iacob castle, and the 1759 doublet in the Bekaa ($M_W$ ~6.7 and ~7.4) destroyed Baalbek and added 0.5 m of displacement at the same site [@Wechsler2014DSF; @Meghraoui2015DSF]. The largest instrumental event of the DST and the Red Sea is the 22 November 1995 Gulf of Aqaba earthquake ($M_W$ 7.2), a multi-segment left-lateral strike-slip event that caused damage in Nuweiba, Eilat, and Aqaba, a small tsunami, and liquefaction, with aftershocks until 1997 [@Klinger1999Aqaba; @Baer2008Nuweiba].

In the active shallow crust of the Zagros and Iran, the 1909 Dorud earthquake ($M_S$ 7.4) on the Main Recent Fault is the largest twentieth-century event of that structure and anchors its recurrence [@Tchalenko1974Dorud; @Talebian2002MRF; @Authemayou2006Zagros]. The 2003 Bam earthquake ($M_W$ 6.6), a right-lateral strike-slip event on a blind fault unmapped before the event, caused between 26,000 and 31,000 deaths without producing surface rupture despite the shallow displacement [@Talebian2004Bam; @Tatar2005Bam; @BerberianBamIranica]. The 2005 Qeshm Island event ($M_W$ 5.8–6.0), of pure reverse mechanism, connects the buried thrust with the surface folding of the Simply Folded Belt and calibrates the seismicity of a historically quiet sector [@Nissen2007Qeshm]. The 2017 Sarpol Zahab earthquake ($M_W$ 7.3), a low-angle blind thrust in the basement of the Mountain Front Fault, is the largest instrumental event of the Zagros, with more than 630 deaths and no surface rupture despite its magnitude [@Nissen2019SarpolZahab].

The only large instrumental earthquake of the subduction (SIF) regime is the 1945 Makran event ($M_W$ 8.1), on the eastern segment, which generated a regional tsunami with waves of up to ~10 m and about 4000 casualties, the largest instrumental event recorded on the Makran interface [@Byrne1992Makran; @GEMFaultedEarth]. In the intraplate extensional regime of the peninsula, the 1982 Dhamar (Yemen) earthquake ($M_W$ 6.0–6.2), a double event of conjugate normal faulting separated by ~12 s, is the most destructive of the peninsula in the instrumental era, with about 2500–2800 casualties and ~300 villages destroyed, and was the first south-Arabian event recorded teleseismically by the WWSSN/GDSN networks [@Langer1987Yemen; @Ambraseys1994Arabia; @Alrubaidi2021Yemen]. The largest earthquake recorded in the Gulf of Aden is the 2006 event ($M_W$ 6.6), of extensional–strike-slip ridge mechanism, although its exact date and mechanism have not been verified with a primary source [@Arfa2024SWSaudiPSHA]. The Harrat Lunayyir swarm of April to July 2009 (maximum $M_W$ 5.7), with more than 30,000 earthquakes, 8–14 km of surface faulting, and up to ~1 m of vertical throw, documents the seismogenic activity of the harrat volcanic zones [@Pallister2010Lunayyir; @Jonsson2012InSAR].

The biases of the record are complementary between the pre-instrumental and instrumental periods. The historical catalogue of the Levant and Arabia is long but heterogeneous: a quarter of the EMME historical records lack a quantitative estimate, and completeness for moderate magnitudes is recent outside Iran and Turkey [@Erdik2012EMME; @Zare2014ECME]. The Yemeni historical record includes destructive events documented since 742 CE, but before 1982 the last event with casualties was that of 1941, whose parameters have not been verified [@Ambraseys1994Arabia; @Alrubaidi2021Yemen]. The peninsular interior has practically no historical record of large events, consistent with its treatment as a stable continental region [@Ambraseys1994Arabia; @GEMHazardTeamARB]. For western Makran, the debate over its seismogenic potential —aseismic creep versus a locked megathrust with long recurrence— remains open and its historical record is poor and disputed [@Byrne1992Makran].
