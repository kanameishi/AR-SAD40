The adopted source model is a local implementation of the *2023 National Seismic Hazard Assessment for Australia* (NSHA23), developed by Geoscience Australia. The model overview documents its scientific characterization and expert weighting, while the data record publishes the OpenQuake input files required to reproduce it [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; @AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data]. The adopted implementation retains that structure and normalizes tectonic region types (TRTs) to the codes used in the calculation: ASC for active or non-cratonic shallow crust, SCC for stable or cratonic continental crust, SIF for subduction interface, and SIS for intraslab seismicity.

NSHA23 combines alternative representations of Australian seismicity because each model family resolves a different spatial scale. Background area sources distribute occurrence within broad zones; seismotectonic models combine regional zonations with the national fault-source model; and smoothed-seismicity models distribute observed rates over point-source grids. Plate-margin sources extend the domain toward Indonesia and Papua New Guinea to represent crustal, interface, and intraslab earthquakes capable of affecting northern Australia [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; @AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data].

The adopted logic tree contains 16 source-model-selection branches. Five represent background models—NSHM12 Background, Leonard, two ARUP alternatives, and Neotectonic Domains—; three combine AUS6, DIM-AUS, and NSHM12 with the national fault-source model; and eight combine four smoothed-seismicity formulations with faults, each with conventional and truncated-window declustering alternatives. Branch weights range from 0.0203 to 0.1997 and sum to one. Area and seismotectonic branches use the collapsed representation published with NSHA23, while smoothed-seismicity branches encode incremental rates for each point source. Recurrence and maximum-magnitude uncertainty is integrated into those rates before the calculation instead of producing an additional logic-tree level [@AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data].

Area sources represent background, regional, and plate-margin zones; point sources materialize the smoothed-seismicity grids; simple faults represent crustal structures in the national fault-source model; and complex faults describe three-dimensional subduction-margin surfaces. Faults are integrated additively with distributed seismicity: NSHA23 treats them as independent of the historical seismicity used to characterize the regional zones [@AllenEtAl2023NSHA23; @AllenEtAl2024NSHA23Data].

@tbl-ssm-aus-inventory summarizes the source identities in the complete local inventory. The count consolidates a provider identifier that appears in multiple branches; it therefore represents unique sources rather than repeated objects summed over the 16 realizations.^[The source count does not represent relative hazard contribution. Contribution depends on occurrence rates, branch weights, source-to-site distances, and the ground-motion distribution.] The ASC/SCC category identifies sources whose TRT assignment changes between the two codes with the selected branch.
