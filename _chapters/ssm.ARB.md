The adopted seismic source model is a local adaptation of the seismic source component of the regional Arabian Peninsula hazard model (ARB), developed by the Saudi Geological Survey (SGS) and translated by the Global Earthquake Model Foundation (GEM) into OpenQuake format.^[A seismic source represents the location, geometry, and recurrence of the earthquakes considered; OpenQuake derives from it the ruptures used in the calculation [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].] The GEM report documents version v2018.2.0, lists 8 June 2023 as the processing date, and defines the original domain as Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, and Qatar [@GEMHazardTeamARB].

The version history records the incorporation, for the western Zagros in Iran, of two selections from the regional Middle East (MIE) source model, developed within the Earthquake Model of the Middle East (EMME) project. The MIE source-model logic tree contains two alternative branches: an area-source model and a fault-source model combined with smoothed seismicity.^[Smoothed seismicity represents the spatial distribution of seismicity rates as a continuous field derived from the earthquake catalog [@GEMHazardTeamMIE].] ARB incorporates one selection from each branch for sources located within 300 km of the United Arab Emirates [@GEMHazardTeamMIE; @GEMHazardTeamARB]. The scientific characterization refers to the documented version; the branch structure, effective source types, and tectonic region type (TRT) codes correspond to the adopted implementation.

The tectonic framework of the model comprises convergence of the Arabian Plate with Eurasia, thrust faulting in the Zagros and eastern Anatolia, the continuation of this margin as subduction beneath Iran and western Pakistan, strike-slip deformation associated with the Dead Sea Transform, and oceanic spreading in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. These processes define the convergent, strike-slip, and extensional settings that frame the regional source characterization [@GEMHazardTeamARB].

The published source characterization delineates 29 homogeneous area-source zones, each with a common characterization of occurrence, maximum magnitude, depth distribution, and dominant style of faulting [@GEMHazardTeamARB]. In OpenQuake, this characterization is implemented through area sources. A regular mesh distributes nucleation points within the polygon. The magnitude–frequency distribution (MFD)^[An MFD assigns annual occurrence rates to magnitude intervals [@OpenQuakeHazardlib326].], hypocentral depths, and nodal planes^[A nodal plane combines the strike, dip, and rake of a rupture [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].] determine the magnitudes, depths, orientations, and rates of the ruptures [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].^[The polygon bounds the nucleation points; rupture surfaces may extend beyond this boundary [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].]

The two base-model alternatives retain the same zonation and differ in the occurrence parameters obtained using the Gardner–Knopoff and Uhrhammer declustering procedures, which apply different space–time windows [@GEMCatalogueDeclustering; @GEMHazardTeamARB].^[Declustering separates the space–time clusters associated with the same earthquake sequence to estimate rates from the resulting catalog [@GEMCatalogueDeclustering].]

The v2018.1.0 update incorporated into ARB two source selections for the Zagros—one from each branch of the MIE model—and assigned equal weight to both selections. Version v2018.2.0 extended the magnitude range of distributed crustal seismicity down to 4.0 and corrected the symmetric application of those sources within the logic tree [@GEMHazardTeamARB]. A logic tree represents alternative interpretations or values through weighted branches.^[Each branch contains an alternative and a relative weight within its branch set. The weight of each path is the product of its branch weights and determines its contribution to the statistical results of the calculation [@OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].]

The adopted implementation comprises twelve source-model branches formed by combining two occurrence-parameter alternatives, two source selections—one from each alternative MIE branch—and three maximum-magnitude perturbation values ($-0.2$, $0.0$, and $+0.2$); each combination constitutes a complete source model. The assigned weights are 0.5/0.5 for the occurrence alternatives, 0.6/0.4 for the selections derived from MIE, and 0.2/0.6/0.2 for the maximum-magnitude perturbations; their products define the twelve branches and sum to unity. The 0.6/0.4 weighting belongs to the adopted implementation. The ARB v2018.2.0 report documents equal weighting for the incorporated MIE selections [@GEMHazardTeamARB].

The effective source types depend on the incorporated MIE source selection. One selection combines area and complex fault sources; the other combines area, multi-point, simple fault, and complex fault sources. Multi-point sources group point sources on a mesh and retain one MFD for each node. Simple fault sources represent surfaces constructed from a fault trace, dip, and depth limits; complex fault sources use three-dimensional edges capable of following changes in geometry along the structure [@OpenQuakeHazardlib326; @OpenQuakeSourceModels326].

The current branches assign sources to the ASC (Active Shallow Crust), SCC (Stable Continental Crust), SIF (Subduction Interface), and SIS (Intraslab and Deep Seismicity) TRT codes.^[The TRT is the attribute through which OpenQuake selects the set of ground-motion models applicable to each source [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326].] Recurrence is represented by truncated Gutenberg–Richter distributions, defined by activity parameter $a$, slope $b$, and the magnitude bounds $M_{\min}$ and $M_{\max}$, which determine annual rates by magnitude interval. In multi-point sources, `MultiMFD` holds one distribution of this family for each node in a single structure [@OpenQuakeHazardlib326]. The local tree therefore represents three axes of uncertainty: the $M_{\max}$ perturbation, the occurrence alternatives, and the source selections drawn from the MIE branches.

The TRT assignment provides the computational link between the tectonics represented by ARB and the ground-motion prediction chapter: for each source, OpenQuake applies the ground-motion model (GMM) branches associated with its TRT and combines their weights with those of the source-model tree [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326; @OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].

@tbl-ssm-arb-inventory summarizes the 104 unique source identities in the complete local inventory. The count consolidates each `providerID` across the twelve source-model branches and reports it once; it therefore represents unique sources rather than repeated branch or site records.^[The source count is a structural inventory, not a measure of relative hazard contribution. Contribution depends on occurrence rates, branch weights, source-to-site distances, and the ground-motion distribution.]
