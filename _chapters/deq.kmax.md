ICOLD and FEMA frame the evaluation of dams and embankments through successive tiers of analysis: pseudostatic checks, simplified deformation estimates, and dynamic analyses when the consequence, the anticipated response, or the complexity of the mechanism requires them [@ICOLD2016; @FEMA2005]. Within this framework, the Newmark method provides a deformation-based estimate and complements the limit-equilibrium verification; it does not turn the pseudostatic coefficient into a universal property of the structure type.

The USACE manuals EM 1110-2-1902 and EM 1110-2-2100 include pseudostatic analysis among the methods applicable to dams and earthworks and address the sliding block for estimating permanent seismic deformation. The selection of the coefficient is tied to the site-specific hazard, the potential failure mechanism, and the performance criteria of the project; the manuals do not establish a single value applicable to all conditions [@USACE2003; @USACE2005].

Finally, NCHRP Report 611 formulates the seismic analysis of slopes and transportation infrastructure in terms of displacement-based performance and uses simplified Newmark correlations to relate demand to permanent deformation [@AndersonEtAl2008]. Eurocode 8 ties the pseudostatic action to the design seismic acceleration and the consequence class, and admits Newmark displacement calculations when the ratio between the critical acceleration and the design acceleration justifies a deformation-based verification [@CEN2004]. These references provide framing and comparison criteria; their indicative values do not substitute for the displacement objective defined for the project.

The coefficients of this report are read against four damage thresholds of the relative permanent displacement, $D_a/H$ = `r paste(.fmt(DAMAGE.CUTS, 1), collapse = ", ")`%: the crest-settlement ratios that Aliberti, Biondi, Cascone and Rampello derive from ninety case histories of earthquake damage to embankment dams and associate with the Operational, Damage, Life Safety and Collapse limit states of the Italian dam code NTD14 [@Aliberti2019]. Their denominator, the embankment height plus the thickness of the deformable foundation, is the slope height used here. The coefficient tables are reported at those thresholds and the coefficient figures shade the intervals they delimit.
