The distribution of each scenario integrates three sources of uncertainty. Epistemic rupture uncertainty is represented by the explicit physical variants adopted for the hypocentral depth, the dip, the rupture style, and the plane orientation; the $N_R$ realizations receive the same weight $1/N_R$. Epistemic model uncertainty represents the differences among the GMM branches admitted for the tectonic regime of the scenario; each branch enters with the weight $v_g$ defined by the adopted logic tree. Aleatory variability represents the record-to-record dispersion for a fixed rupture realization and GMM branch. In component $k=(r,g)$ it is expressed through the logarithmic standard deviation $\sigma_k$ of branch $g$, evaluated for realization $r$. The combination $w_k=v_g/N_R$ simultaneously incorporates the rupture variation and the model weighting in the mixture of [-@eq-scenario-mixture]. The dispersion among the medians $\mu_k$ characterizes the two epistemic contributions, while each $\sigma_k$ contributes the aleatory variability of its component. The resulting distribution is characterized through the fractiles of [-@eq-scenario-moments], and each component preserves the correspondence among the rupture geometry, the source-to-site distances, and the GMM branch that originated its prediction.
