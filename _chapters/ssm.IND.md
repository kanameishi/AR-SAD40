
The seismic source model (SSM) defines the spatial distribution, geometry, and earthquake occurrence rates of all seismogenic sources considered capable of contributing to ground-motion hazard at a site. In the hazard integral ([-@eq-hazard-integral]), the SSM provides the magnitude-frequency distributions $f_{M,s}(m)$, the spatial distributions $f_{\mathbf{R}|M,s}(\mathbf{r}|m)$, the occurrence rates $\nu_0^{(s)}$, and the maximum magnitude $M_{\max}^{(s)}$ for each source $s$ [@Cornell1968; @SSHAC1997]. Epistemic uncertainty in these parameters is captured through the source-model logic tree described in [@sec-ssm].

The IND model corresponds to the probabilistic seismic hazard model for the Indian subcontinent developed by Nath and Thingbaijam (2012) [-@NathThingbaijam2012], subsequently updated and translated into OpenQuake format in collaboration with Natural Resources Canada (version v2012.2.0), as documented in the Global Earthquake Model Foundation (GEM) model report [@GEMHazardTeamIND]. The model domain extends from approximately $60.0^\circ$E to $100.8^\circ$E in longitude and from $2.0^\circ$N to $40.0^\circ$N in latitude, covering India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Pakistan, Myanmar, and surrounding regions of the Indian subcontinent. The implementation is publicly available at the `nackerley/indian-subcontinent-psha` repository on GitHub [@NackerleyRepo].

The inventory comprises 443,197 sources of two types: 108 area sources (`areaSource`) and 443,089 point sources (`pointSource`) distributed over two smoothed-seismicity grids.^[Smoothed seismicity represents the spatial distribution of seismic rates through a continuous field derived from the catalogue, materialised as a regular grid of point sources.] Sources are classified under four tectonic region types (TRTs)^[In OpenQuake, the TRT associates each source with the set of ground-motion models (GMMs) defined for that setting in the ground-motion logic tree [@OpenQuakeSourceModels326; @OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].]: Active Shallow Crust (ASC), Stable Continental Crust (SCC), Subduction Interface (SIF), and Subduction Intraslab (SIS). The minimum magnitude adopted for hazard calculations is $M_w$ 4.5 across all area sources; the two smoothed-seismicity grids employ $M_{\min}$ thresholds of 4.5 and 5.5, respectively.

The source-model logic tree encodes epistemic uncertainty through three branch sets that produce $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$ end-branches.^[Each branch carries an alternative and a relative weight within its set. The weight of each path is the product of the weights of its branches and determines its contribution to the statistical results of the calculation [@OpenQuakeLogicTrees326].] The first branch set selects the source model among three realisations of the regional seismicity: branch `b1m1` (weight 0.40) adopts the 108-zone areal model, whereas branches `b1m2` (weight 0.27) and `b1m3` (weight 0.33) adopt the spatially smoothed point-source grids with $M_{\min}$ thresholds of 4.5 and 5.5, respectively. The second branch set (type `maxMagGRRelative`) applies simultaneous relative perturbations of $-0.3$, $0.0$, and $+0.3$ to the base $M_{\max}$ of all sources (branches `b2m1`, `b2m2`, and `b2m3`, with weights 0.32, 0.36, and 0.32); the third branch set applies the same symmetric weight structure to Gutenberg–Richter $b$-value perturbations of amplitude $\mp 0.1$ (branches `b3m1`, `b3m2`, and `b3m3`). In both perturbation sets, the unperturbed central branch carries the highest weight, reflecting the greater credibility attributed to the nominal parameter estimate, and the weights within each set sum to unity [@GEMHazardTeamIND].

All 108 area sources employ the truncated Gutenberg–Richter distribution (`truncGutenbergRichterMFD`), the only magnitude-frequency distribution (MFD) type present in the model: incremental (`incrementalMFD`) and arbitrary (`arbitraryMFD`) MFDs, `multiMFD` structures, and characteristic or hybrid formulations are absent.^[An MFD assigns annual occurrence rates to magnitude intervals. In a truncated Gutenberg–Richter distribution, parameter $a$ controls the cumulative activity level and $b$ the relative decrease in frequency with increasing magnitude, within the $M_{\min}$ and $M_{\max}$ bounds [@Weichert1980; @OpenQuakeHazardlib326].] The minimum magnitude is $M_w$ 4.5 for area sources in ASC, SCC, and SIF settings; SIS sources encompass $M_{\min}$ values from 4.5 to 7.5, reflecting the variable depth of the deep slab zones. The two smoothed-seismicity grids inherit the Gutenberg–Richter parametrisation of the parent areal zones: each grid node carries an $a$-value spatially redistributed from the rates of the enclosing zone, rather than an independent fit to catalogue statistics [@NathThingbaijam2012].

Base $M_{\max}$ values for the 108 area sources range from 6.0 to 9.4 across the four TRTs, spanning environments from stable continental crust to subduction megathrusts. The highest values correspond to Subduction Interface zones — the Sumatra and Himalaya–Main Frontal Thrust systems — where great earthquakes exceeding $M$ 8.5 are considered possible. Values are assigned per zone from the seismotectonic characterisation study by Nath and Thingbaijam (2012) [-@NathThingbaijam2012] and are set as explicit parameters within the `truncGutenbergRichterMFD` specification; their epistemic uncertainty corresponds to the $\pm 0.3$ perturbation of the second branch set described above. In the smoothed-seismicity grids, $M_{\max}$ is inherited from the parent areal-zone parametrisation and follows the same global perturbation scheme, without independent constraint per point source.

Three magnitude-area scaling relationships relate moment magnitude to rupture dimensions, assigned by tectonic environment. The Wells and Coppersmith (1994) [-@WellsCoppersmith1994] relationship (`WC1994`) is applied to the 46 area sources in ASC and SCC settings; the Strasser et al. (2010) [-@Strasser2010] intraslab relationship (`StrasserIntraslab`), to the 35 SIS sources; and the interface relationship of the same authors (`StrasserInterface`), to the 27 SIF sources, so that $46 + 35 + 27 = 108$ accounts for all area sources. All area sources adopt a rupture aspect ratio of 2.0.

The seismogenic depth model reflects a multi-layer structure consistent with the tectonic environment of each zone. ASC sources are confined to the upper 25 km, with upper seismogenic depth $z_{\text{upper}}$ of 0 km, lower seismogenic depth $z_{\text{lower}}$ of 25 km, and mean hypocentral depth of 15 km. SCC sources span $z_{\text{upper}}$ of 0 to 25 km and $z_{\text{lower}}$ of 25 to 70 km, with mean hypocentral depths of 15 to 25 km. SIF sources exhibit $z_{\text{upper}}$ of 0 to 70 km and $z_{\text{lower}}$ of 25 to 180 km, with mean hypocentral depths of 15 to 70 km. SIS sources occupy the deepest zone, with $z_{\text{upper}}$ of 25 to 180 km, $z_{\text{lower}}$ of 70 to 300 km, and mean hypocentral depths of 25 to 180 km. Hypocentral depth distributions are specified as single-value distributions with probability 1.0 for all area sources; that is, a single representative hypocentral depth is assigned deterministically to each zone. The resulting architecture comprises three layers: shallow crustal sources (ASC and the upper SCC layer) confined to the uppermost 25 km; intermediate-depth zones (lower SCC and the upper portions of the subduction envelopes) extending to 70 km; and deep subduction intraslab zones reaching 300 km. The wide SIF and SIS ranges reflect the variability of the subduction geometries included in the domain, from shallow interface contact zones to deep ruptures within the downgoing slab.

The IND model contains no fault sources: the inventory consists exclusively of the 108 areal seismogenic zones and the 443,089 point sources of the two smoothed-seismicity grids, with no fault-source XML files and no slip-rate data assigned to any source. Accordingly, the seismic-moment balance $\dot{M}_0 = \mu\, A\, S$^[The seismic moment rate $\dot{M}_0$ is related to the shear modulus $\mu$, the fault area $A$, and the long-term slip rate $S$; this relationship connects geodetic or geologic slip-rate estimates to the moment release rate used in MFD construction.] plays no role in the parametrisation of this model, and the recurrence parameters of all area sources derive from earthquake-catalogue statistics via Gutenberg–Richter fitting [@NathThingbaijam2012; @GEMHazardTeamIND].
