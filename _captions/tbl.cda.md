CDA Seismic Design Criteria by Consequence Classification. Source: CDA Technical Bulletin, Application of Dam Safety Guidelines to Mining Dams [-@cda2021], Table 3-3 (construction, operation and transition) and Table 4-2 (closure, passive care). The Canadian Dam Association (CDA) defines five consequence classifications, assigned from population at risk, loss of life, and environmental, cultural and economic losses. The tabulated levels are target levels for initial consideration and consultation between owner and regulator, not fixed requirements: the designer of record and the owner may adopt more stringent levels to further reduce risk, or less stringent ones where justified. The maximum credible earthquake (MCE) is a declared alternative to the probabilistic level, not an upper bound; CDA notes only that the MCE has no associated annual exceedance probability. Passive care is not abandonment: CDA requires a steady-state condition, demonstrated by accumulated experience and monitoring, before a facility enters that phase. CDA does not use OBE/SEE terminology; it specifies an Earthquake Design Ground Motion (EDGM).
